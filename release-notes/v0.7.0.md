Seventh release of nia-edge, the client of Nia. It is a pre-release. It follows Nia's new policy editor: a tool can be reviewed in every conversation, and the machine tells the console what it holds, so that its catalogue is filled in a tap.

**New**
- **A tool reviewed in every conversation.** A role's tool may say `when_clean: require_approval` (or `deny`): its calls wait for a reviewer, or are refused, even before the conversation read anything from outside. A conversation that did takes the stricter of `when_clean` and `when_untrusted`. This is what the editor's *Normally* column and an Internet rule of *Ask me* for a new site need.
- **The machine's inventory.** The hourly scan (`nia-edge-agents.timer`, as root, without network) also lists the machine's systemd services with their state, and the names under `/etc` to four levels, each marked private when other users cannot read it. Never a file's content, never a link followed. It is written to `/var/lib/nia-edge/inventory.json` (root and the nia-edge group only) and sent once per scan to a Nia that takes it. The console's Machine section offers them as services to allow and paths to make writable, protected or secret. It decides nothing.

**Upgrade from 0.6.0**

`sudo sh update-linux.sh v0.7.0`, then `sudo nia-edge agents` once: it writes the first inventory, and the next heartbeat sends it, without waiting an hour. If the machine has no `nia-edge-agents.timer` yet (installed before 0.6.0, and `install/linux.sh` of 0.6.0 not run since), run `install/linux.sh` of v0.7.0 once, with `NIA_EDGE_BINARY` pointing at the new binary.

Update every machine of a tenant before its policy uses `when_clean`: a nia-edge 0.6.0 or older refuses such a policy whole, and with it every call (it fails closed). The console's review says which machines are too old before you publish.

**Not yet**
- Lists of sites fetched from a URL (URLhaus, CrowdSec), and catalogue paths outside `/etc`.
- Governing on macOS and Windows; Codex's commands simulated inside its own sandbox.

**Verify what you download**

```
sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS
```

The builds are reproducible: `scripts/release.sh v0.7.0` on this tag, with Go 1.27.4, gives the same checksums.
